# Curveballs & Mid-Case Injections

Use these to escalate difficulty mid-case, test adaptability, or stress-test a candidate's recommendation.

---

## Category 1: New Data That Changes the Picture

**Contradiction inject:**
> "Actually, before you continue — I just heard back from the engineering team. They DID push a deploy on Tuesday, but it was a backend change. No frontend code was touched. Does that change your hypothesis?"

**Denominator reveal:**
> "One more thing: the week you're analyzing had 40% higher traffic than normal due to a paid campaign. Does that affect your interpretation?"

**Segment flip:**
> "You said mobile is likely the issue. I just pulled the data: mobile conversion is actually UP 5%. It's desktop that's down 31%. How do you adjust?"

**Timeline constraint:**
> "The client needs a preliminary answer for a board meeting in 90 minutes. You can run one more query. What do you look at?"

---

## Category 2: Stakeholder / Political Complication

**Conflicting stakeholders:**
> "The CMO is convinced it's a paid channel issue. The VP Product is convinced it's the new UI. They both have data that supports their view. How do you navigate this?"

**Findings challenged:**
> "Opposing counsel is going to argue that your anomaly detection methodology is arbitrary — why did you pick 3 standard deviations as the threshold? How do you defend that?"

**Executive override:**
> "The CEO has already told the press that the drop is 'temporary and seasonal.' Your data doesn't support that. What do you do?"

---

## Category 3: Data / Methodology Problems

**Missing data:**
> "Turns out 15% of your transactions are missing a key field due to a logging bug. How does this affect your analysis and what do you do about it?"

**Sample bias reveal:**
> "The A/B test was only run on users who had logged in within the last 30 days. How does that affect the validity of the results?"

**Causality vs correlation:**
> "You've shown a strong correlation between Feature X usage and retention. The PM wants to put Feature X on the homepage. What's the risk of that recommendation?"

---

## Category 4: Scope Expansion

**Scope creep:**
> "While you were analyzing this, the CFO pulled you aside and asked you to also look at why support tickets are up 18% this month. How do you handle competing priorities?"

**Upstream / downstream:**
> "Your analysis identified the drop is in the checkout funnel. But your manager wants to know: is this a symptom, or could it be causing the revenue drop you'll see next quarter? How do you think about that?"

**Scale question:**
> "This is working in one market. The CEO wants to know if this methodology can scale to all 12 markets by Q3. What are the constraints?"

---

## Category 5: Technical Depth Escalation

**Schema question:**
> "Walk me through how you'd actually structure the BigQuery query for the retention analysis you described. What tables, what joins, any gotchas?"

**Model critique:**
> "Your regression shows the coefficient on X is significant. But the R² is 0.18. Does your model actually tell you anything useful?"

**Pipeline question:**
> "If this analysis needs to run daily, automated, and needs to handle late-arriving data, how does your approach change?"

---

## When to Deploy Curveballs

- After candidate reaches a confident early conclusion (test if they can pivot)
- When candidate is too narrow (introduce orthogonal data)
- When candidate is too broad (force prioritization)
- At the 60-70% mark of a drill session (simulate real mid-case pivot)
- When candidate's methodology seems weak — probe with a specific technical challenge

## Calibration Note

Don't pile on multiple curveballs at once. One at a time. Good candidates will:
1. Acknowledge the new information without defensiveness
2. Explicitly state what changes and what doesn't
3. Adjust recommendation proportionally — not do a 180°

A candidate who completely reverses on one new data point is just as problematic as one who ignores it.
